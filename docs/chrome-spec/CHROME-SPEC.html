Afterimage│Chrome design specification│rev A

One machine, and the interface drawn on its own tubes

Everything here is a decision with a value attached. Where I had to choose, I have said what the rule was, so the next decision can be derived instead of re-litigated. Two directions are specified: A — Set-in modules, the fix inside the concept you asked for, fully valued; and B — One tube, the honest alternative, argued but not fully valued. A is my recommendation.

01

The verdict, bluntly

The concept is not the problem. "Rugged housing, flat CRT modules set into it, TUI drawn on the tubes" is a good idea and it is buildable. What went wrong is measurable, and it is four things.

Fault 01 · the value ramp is inverted

The housing is #1c1d21 (L* ≈ 11.5). The screens are #0a141d (L* ≈ 7.0). A gap of 4.5 L* is below the threshold at which the eye reads two materials. Worse, an unlit hole in a lighter case is exactly what a switched-off monitor looks like. Nothing in the current build says the tubes are powered.

Worse still, the bezel was lit on its top edge and cast a shadow below — both raised-object cues, which is why the tubes sit on the case instead of in it. Fix: housing up to #26282e, tube down to #050b11, and invert the bezel into a countersink. See the boundary artboard.

Fault 02 · there is no TUI, only a costume

Inverse video on one element and separator rules between tabs is not a system. A TUI is a grammar: a frame that encloses a region, a title set into that frame, focus expressed by the frame's weight, selection expressed by inversion, keys advertised in the bottom rule, values aligned in columns. Ten pieces of vocabulary, applied everywhere, without exception.

Fix: the frame, specified glyph by glyph in the grammar sheet and §B7.

Fault 03 · one size of one grey

Nearly every chrome label is 13.5 px 3270 in #8ba6bd. Individually that clears AA at 7.3:1 — so contrast is not the failure. Sameness is. With no second step, the eye has no entry point and reads the whole pane as texture.

Fix: a seven-step chrome scale, C1–C7, each with a stated job, spanning #ffffff to #6b8195 and 11.5px to 15px. §B6.

Fault 04 · the empty regions are empty

Two thirds of the left sidebar and most of every right-hand pane is unpainted nothing. On a real terminal there is no such thing as unpainted: the frame runs to the corner, the bottom rule carries the key hints, and the space inside is deliberate overscan. Right now it reads as a half-built app, which is also the reason the single-icon dock strip "looks broken" — it isn't the strip, it's the absence of a frame to belong to.

Fix: the frame closes every region, and the focused pane's bottom rule carries its keys. Degradation.

Two things I would delete outright. The drifting refresh bar: a moving object on a 9-second cycle is invisible for a week and intolerable in month two, and it is consuming the third compositing layer you need for the tube stack. And the centre phosphor bloom as currently tuned: at its present strength it is doing no perceptual work while costing a full-pane gradient. Keep the bloom, raise it, and put the saved layer into the glass sheen.

02

The boundary rule

The principle

Housing is what the machine is. Screen is what the machine is showing.

Operationally: if it changes when you open a different note, it is screen. That single test settles every element in the inventory, including the dock tabs, which change with what you are looking at and are therefore screen — the same tube, inside the same bezel, under the same raster. It is why they have been flip-flopped three times: a control that looks like hardware but behaves like content will never sit still.

Two corollaries, both load-bearing. One: housing and screen never meet without a bezel — there is no such thing as a flush join, because a flush join is what made the darker sub-panel behind the tab strip read as a seam. Two: housing never lights up, with exactly one exception — the ribbon's open-dock indicator, which is a recessed lens over an LED and is allowed to be one.

Three — the same rule decides controls, which is the metaphor. There are exactly two kinds of control on this machine and you can tell them apart without reading anything. Keys are moulded, physical, and on the case; they operate the machine — which panel is open, settings — and they light a lens when engaged. Fields are drawn, bracketed, and on the tube; they operate content — new note, sort, collapse — and the bracket is the button, exactly as [ OK ] is a button in dialog(1). A control's shape tells you what it acts on before you read its glyph. Nothing on the ribbon ever gets brackets; nothing on a tube ever gets a keycap.

03

Direction A — Set-in modules

Five flat tubes set into a machined case, each one a framed Turbo Vision window drawn in its own phosphor. The case is lifted and the tubes are dropped so the two read as different materials; each tube spills a little light onto the case around it, which is the whole of what says powered. Everything inside a bezel is drawn with rules, glyphs and inverse video. Nothing inside a bezel is a physical object, and nothing outside one is a drawn one.

Artboard 2│The housing / screen boundary · 8×
Correction to the previous round

The bezel carried a 1px catch light along its top edge and an outward drop shadow beneath the module. Both of those are raised-object cues: a thing that sticks out of a surface is lit on top, shaded below, and throws a shadow onto whatever it sits on. A hole is the exact inverse. Its top wall faces away from the light and goes dark; its bottom wall faces the light and goes bright; and it casts nothing, anywhere. That inversion is the entire reason the modules read as panels lying on the case rather than screens set into it — and it is one gradient and one deletion to fix.

Eight layers across roughly 6 real pixels. The load-bearing one is the countersink wall: a 4px chamfer running dark at the top to light at the bottom, painted as a border-box gradient behind a transparent border. It is the only layer that states which way is up, and once it is right the module cannot be read as sitting on top of anything.

Artboard 3│Tab strip · every state · 3×

Selection is carried by silhouette first, rule weight second, brightness third. The active tab is the place where the frame's top rule breaks open into the pane below it — a shape change, which survives 20% zoom, greyscale, and every one of the five accent presets. It paints no fill, so it cannot punch a hole in the tube's glow.

No accent anywhere in the tab strip. A coloured bar across the top of the selected tab is the house style of every IDE and browser shipped since about 2015, and it is the one detail that would tell anyone looking that this is a modern app wearing a costume. A terminal never had it. The rule around the notch lifting from #44637c to #7fa8c4 says the same thing in the vocabulary the machine already speaks — and it costs no hue, so all five presets get it free. Accent is now confined to four places in the whole chrome: hotkey glyphs, the :focus-visible ring, the ribbon's indicator lens, and a status value that is itself the subject.

04

Direction B — One tube

The honest alternative, and the thing I would build if the gutters keep failing. One CRT fills the whole window. The housing is reduced to a 16px bezel around the window edge plus the ribbon rail. Panes are divided not by case but by box-drawing rules on the same continuous raster — which is what a real terminal actually does. Norton Commander is one screen with two framed panels; tmux is one screen with a status line.

For
  • The raster is continuous across the entire window, which passes the 20% test with no help from value contrast at all.
  • No dark gutters. Roughly 4% more usable area, and the sidebars stop looking half-empty.
  • The TUI becomes structural rather than applied — the rules are the only thing dividing anything, so the grammar cannot be skipped.
  • Substantially less CSS, and no per-module bezel stack to keep in sync.
Against
  • It contradicts "leave space around the screens", and it loses the gadget quality — five modules in a machined case is simply more machine than one flat panel.
  • One unbroken raster across 1600px is more insistent than five smaller ones. Over eight hours that matters.
  • To be truly continuous, no pane may ever paint a fill — including modals, the graph view and any plugin that assumes it can. That constraint is permanent.
My call

Build A. Its failure was never the concept; it was that the case and the tubes were the same value and nothing said "powered". Both are one-line fixes. Keep B in your pocket: if you make the changes in §B10 and it still reads as an app rather than a machine, the gutters are the thing to delete, not the frame.

Part B

The spec

Values for Direction A, dark mode, at 1× on a 100% display. Every colour is either a fixed neutral or a stated relationship to --after-accent / --after-text, so all five presets work unchanged. §B1 is §02 above.

The column that stops the regression. Default is PAINTS NOTHING. A surface on a tube reads as a card sitting on a screen, with a seam, which is exactly what broke twice. Three rules that go with the stack. One: both pseudo-elements carry pointer-events:none — no overlay may intercept a pointer. Two: the module must be a stacking context (position:relative; isolation:isolate) or multiply will blend against the housing instead of the tube and the bezel will go black. B6

The chrome type scale

Seven steps. Size carries hierarchy between C1 and C4 — the pane title is deliberately larger than the row text beneath it, which is the inversion of most app chrome and the thing that makes a terminal feel like a terminal. Colour carries state within a step, never across one. Face carries kind: 3270 for anything the machine says about itself, Courier Prime for anything that came out of your vault.

B9

Motion

Nothing in the chrome moves on its own. Ever.

This is the cheap answer and it is also the right one. A machine that is idle is idle. The drifting refresh bar is the single thing in the current build most likely to be switched off by a real user in week three, it costs a compositing layer you need, and it undermines the theme's own claim — that this stays interesting while completely motionless.

B10

Build order

If you only get three in, take 1, 2 and 3. They are also the three cheapest.

1
THE VALUE RAMP + THE SPILL

Housing to #26282e with the ramp, tube to #050b11, and a 34px ink-tinted spill on every module. Four token changes and one box-shadow. This is the change that fixes "dark panels on a dark background", and it fixes it completely.

2
THE FRAME

1px inset frame on every tab group, pane title set into the top rule, 3px double on focus. Roughly forty lines, and it is the entire difference between "app with a filter" and "TUI".

3
THE TYPE RAMP

C1–C7 in place of the one dim grey. Largest legibility gain per byte in the whole document, and it is the fault the owner keeps describing as "too small" — the problem was never the size, it was that nothing was bigger.

4
TABS AS NOTCHES

Retire the solid accent block. Silhouette-based selection, no fill, one knockout strip.

5
THE STATUS TUBE

Its own module. High gadget-per-line ratio and the bottom of the window stops being dead.

6
BRACKETS · KEY HINTS · LEADERS · NAMEPLATE

Polish, in that order. Delete the refresh bar whenever you touch the tube stack.

B12

What CSS on Obsidian's DOM cannot do

TWO PSEUDO-ELEMENTS, NO MORE

The tube needs two over-content layers and you have exactly two. Fallback: deleting the refresh bar is what makes this work. If you insist on a third layer, hang it on .workspace-tab-container — but it will not cover the tab strip.

TITLES CANNOT MOVE INTO THE FRAME

A true TUI sets the title in the top rule. You cannot reparent .view-header-title. Fallback: the header row becomes a rule — ┤ TITLE ├ then a 1px flex-fill to the frame edge. Visually identical, and it is what Artboard 1 shows.

COUNTS CANNOT BE HOISTED

The 14 in the FILES rule cannot be read out of the tree below it. Fallback: where a count exists in the DOM (backlinks, tags, search) it already sits in .tree-item-flair and can be styled in place. Where it does not, omit it — do not fake it.

KEY HINTS ARE STATIC TEXT

CSS cannot read the user's keymap. ^N would be a hard-coded string. Fallback: ship hints only for bindings Obsidian does not let the user rebind away, or make the whole row a Style Settings toggle, default on, with a one-line caveat. Do not invent shortcuts.

MULTIPLY NEEDS A STACKING CONTEXT

Without isolation: isolate on the module, the scan layer blends against the housing and the bezel goes black. This is the failure that reads as "the effect disappeared".

THE EDITING SAFETY RULES STILL BIND

.cm-content stays position: static; nothing here touches it. Both tube layers are pointer-events: none. No chrome element clips, filters or recolours editable text, and the module raster is deliberately a separate, coarser system from the body-text raster — do not merge them.
